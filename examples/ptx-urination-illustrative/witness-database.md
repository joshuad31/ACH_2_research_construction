# Witness database

**ILLUSTRATIVE RETROSPECTIVE EXAMPLE — NOT A REAL ACH RUN.** Reconstructed from the existing corpus to demonstrate compact outputs. Questions and selection use hindsight. These records are for architecture research, not clinical conclusions.

Raw witnesses only: **60 words maximum**, with exactly one question and one validated-FRD parent per witness. The linked question is its controlled-English version. No witness transcoding or hypothesis coverage calculation was performed. Witness count is not a count of independent studies.

## C01 Ptx human evidence

### W0001

Question: [IQ0001](question-database.md#iq0001) · Validated FRD: [V0001](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0001) · 42 words

The pentoxifylline add-on trial lists prostate size among its measurements but reports only baseline prostate volume. No post-treatment prostate-volume change is provided. The reported symptom and flow improvements therefore cannot establish prostate shrinkage, and missing reporting is not proof of unchanged size.

### W0002

Question: [IQ0002](question-database.md#iq0002) · Validated FRD: [V0002](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0002) · 37 words

In a 60-man, 12-week randomized trial of benign prostatic hyperplasia, pentoxifylline was added to tamsulosin and compared with placebo plus tamsulosin. There was no pentoxifylline-alone arm. The trial therefore estimates an add-on effect, not pentoxifylline monotherapy efficacy.

### W0003

Question: [IQ0003](question-database.md#iq0003) · Validated FRD: [V0003](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0003) · 44 words

In 60 men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, mean total prostate symptom score fell 8.73 points with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin versus 5.13 with placebo plus tamsulosin at 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). This is an add-on symptom effect, not a direct measure of detrusor contractility.

### W0004

Question: [IQ0004](question-database.md#iq0004) · Validated FRD: [V0004](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0004) · 38 words

In men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, mean voiding symptom subscore decreased 5.99 points with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin versus 3.65 with placebo plus tamsulosin over 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). These reported symptoms do not identify the physiological mechanism.

### W0005

Question: [IQ0005](question-database.md#iq0005) · Validated FRD: [V0005](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0005) · 43 words

In men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, mean storage symptom subscore decreased 2.74 points with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin versus 1.48 with placebo plus tamsulosin at 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). This combined frequency, urgency, and nocturia score does not establish improvement in every item.

### W0006

Question: [IQ0006](question-database.md#iq0006) · Validated FRD: [V0006](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0006) · 40 words

In men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, the urinary quality-of-life index decreased by means of 1.96 with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin and 1.30 with placebo plus tamsulosin at 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). This is urinary-condition burden, not a general health utility.

### W0007

Question: [IQ0007](question-database.md#iq0007) · Validated FRD: [V0007](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0007) · 41 words

In men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, mean maximum urinary flow increased 4.48 mL/s with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin versus 2.67 with placebo plus tamsulosin over 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). Flow was measured noninvasively and does not independently identify a contractility mechanism.

### W0008

Question: [IQ0008](question-database.md#iq0008) · Validated FRD: [V0008](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0008) · 45 words

In men with benign prostatic hyperplasia, mean post-void residual urine decreased 32.93 mL with pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin versus 13.17 with placebo plus tamsulosin at 12 weeks (reported between-group p<.001). Baseline means differed, 77.27 versus 50.37 mL, so the change and endpoint contrasts must remain distinct.

### W0009

Question: [IQ0009](question-database.md#iq0009) · Validated FRD: [V0009](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0009) · 39 words

Voided volume showed no statistically significant improvement in either trial arm: mean changes were +0.80 and +1.34 mL for pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin and placebo plus tamsulosin. Table 4 reports p=.22 between groups; within-group p values were .90 and .89.

### W0010

Question: [IQ0010](question-database.md#iq0010) · Validated FRD: [V0010](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0010) · 35 words

The male pentoxifylline add-on trial compared baseline with week 12. It does not establish when benefit first appeared or whether benefit persisted beyond treatment. The authors identified the small sample and short follow-up as limitations.

### W0011

Question: [IQ0011](question-database.md#iq0011) · Validated FRD: [V0011](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0011) · 38 words

The pentoxifylline add-on trial measured symptom scores, uroflowmetry, and sonographic residual volume. It did not report invasive pressure-flow contractility measures. Its explanation that improved bladder contractile strength caused benefit remains an author interpretation, not a directly measured mechanism.

### W0012

Question: [IQ0012](question-database.md#iq0012) · Validated FRD: [V0012](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0012) · 40 words

The pentoxifylline add-on trial selected men older than 40 with symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia and excluded neurogenic bladder, recent acute retention, residual urine above 250 mL, anticoagulant use, and severe hypotension. Its findings do not directly characterize those excluded populations.

### W0013

Question: [IQ0013](question-database.md#iq0013) · Validated FRD: [V0013](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0013) · 42 words

Mild adverse events were reported in 11 of 30 men receiving pentoxifylline plus tamsulosin and 13 of 30 receiving placebo plus tamsulosin. No severe adverse event, urinary retention episode, or treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was reported during the 12-week trial.

### W0014

Question: [IQ0014](question-database.md#iq0014) · Validated FRD: [V0014](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0014) · 41 words

The English abstract says urge incontinence and uninhibited contractions suggested latent cerebrovascular dysfunction. It does not demonstrate that pentoxifylline acted exclusively through cerebral blood flow or establish absence of peripheral urinary effects. The mechanistic attribution remains unproven within this single case.

### W0015

Question: [IQ0015](question-database.md#iq0015) · Validated FRD: [V0015](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0015) · 40 words

In a single 60-year-old woman with uninhibited bladder contractions, incontinence resolved and urinary frequency decreased after two weeks of pentoxifylline. At four weeks, uninhibited contractions were absent and bladder capacity increased. This uncontrolled case provides no male benign-prostatic-hyperplasia efficacy estimate.

## C02 Pelvic floor uncontrolled

### W0021

Among 14 men tested before Clemens’s program, five had detrusor instability, four diminished capacity, and six pseudodyssynergia. None met the paper’s obstruction criterion and no residual volume exceeded 60 mL. These findings describe enrollment phenotype, not treatment-induced changes.

### W0022

Clemens found no significant differences in measured pre- or post-treatment scores when patients were grouped by cystometric abnormality or pseudodyssynergia. This small subgroup analysis did not show predictive value; it does not prove those phenotypes are irrelevant.

## C03 Pelvic floor randomized

### W0044

Question: [IQ0044](question-database.md#iq0044) · Validated FRD: [V0044](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0044) · 39 words

In FitzGerald’s mixed-sex feasibility trial, adverse events were reported in 12 of 23 myofascial-therapy participants versus five of 24 massage participants. Pain was the most common category; two severe pain events occurred with myofascial therapy and one with massage.

### W0045

Question: [IQ0045](question-database.md#iq0045) · Validated FRD: [V0045](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0045) · 41 words

More than 90% of FitzGerald participants identified their assigned treatment at study end, limiting participant blinding. The authors also cautioned that the massage protocol was constrained and did not represent expert routine massage care. General superiority claims exceed this feasibility comparison.

### W0046

Question: [IQ0046](question-database.md#iq0046) · Validated FRD: [V0046](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0046) · 37 words

FitzGerald’s myofascial protocol included pelvic-floor lengthening, relaxation, and trigger-point treatment. Early home exercises were explicitly not Kegel exercises; strengthening could be added later after muscle control. The intervention cannot be equated with an undifferentiated pelvic-floor strengthening prescription.

### W0047

Question: [IQ0047](question-database.md#iq0047) · Validated FRD: [V0047](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0047) · 35 words

FitzGerald counted participants withdrawing before the 12-week assessment as treatment failures in global-response denominators. Three of 47 randomized participants withdrew. This intention-to-treat response rule differs from the available-case denominators used for several numerical symptom endpoints.

### W0048

Question: [IQ0048](question-database.md#iq0048) · Validated FRD: [V0048](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0048) · 37 words

For male chronic pelvic pain, FitzGerald reports mean urinary-subscore changes of −3.9 with myofascial therapy and −0.3 with massage (between-group p=.007). Baseline means differed markedly, 8.9 versus 4.6; 12-week data were available for 11 and nine men.

### W0049

Question: [IQ0049](question-database.md#iq0049) · Validated FRD: [V0049](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0049) · 37 words

Total Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index improved within both male treatment groups: reported mean changes were −14.4 with myofascial therapy and −6.8 with massage. The between-group p value was .07, despite within-group p values of .0003 and .03.

### W0050

Question: [IQ0050](question-database.md#iq0050) · Validated FRD: [V0050](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0050) · 41 words

In FitzGerald’s male subgroup, between-group p values were .09 for the Interstitial Cystitis Symptom Index and .11 for its Problem Index. Thus those outcomes did not establish a statistically significant myofascial-therapy advantage. Their endpoint samples were only seven and six patients.

### W0051

Question: [IQ0051](question-database.md#iq0051) · Validated FRD: [V0051](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0051) · 38 words

In FitzGerald’s male chronic-prostatitis/chronic-pelvic-pain subgroup, seven of 11 myofascial-therapy patients and four of ten massage patients reported moderate or marked improvement. The between-group p value was .39. The small subgroup did not establish a statistically significant global-response difference.

### W0052

Question: [IQ0052](question-database.md#iq0052) · Validated FRD: [V0052](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0052) · 34 words

Glazener reports higher mean costs with formal therapy, approximately £180 after radical prostatectomy and £209 after transurethral resection, without evidence of an economically important quality-adjusted-life-year difference. These are historical trial-setting estimates, not current prices.

### W0053

Question: [IQ0053](question-database.md#iq0053) · Validated FRD: [V0053](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0053) · 42 words

Many Glazener participants already knew pelvic-floor exercises, and intervention participants were more likely to report practicing them at 12 months. The trial compared added formal therapist sessions against usual care in an exercise-aware population, not exercise exposure against complete absence of exercise.

### W0054

Question: [IQ0054](question-database.md#iq0054) · Validated FRD: [V0054](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0054) · 40 words

Glazener found no significant between-group differences in mean incontinence symptom scores at measured timepoints in either surgical trial. At 12 months, adjusted mean differences were −0.34 after radical prostatectomy and −0.04 after transurethral resection, both with confidence intervals crossing zero.

### W0055

Question: [IQ0055](question-database.md#iq0055) · Validated FRD: [V0055](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0055) · 39 words

At 12 months after randomization, persistent incontinence affected 148/196 men assigned formal pelvic-floor therapy and 151/195 controls after radical prostatectomy. The adjusted risk ratio was 0.97 (95% CI 0.87–1.09; p=.64). This trial did not demonstrate an incremental continence benefit.

### W0056

Question: [IQ0056](question-database.md#iq0056) · Validated FRD: [V0056](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0056) · 36 words

At 12 months after randomization following transurethral prostate resection, incontinence affected 126/194 formal-therapy participants and 125/203 controls. The adjusted risk ratio was 1.06 (95% CI 0.91–1.23; p=.47). The trial did not demonstrate an incremental continence benefit.

### W0057

Question: [IQ0057](question-database.md#iq0057) · Validated FRD: [V0057](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0057) · 39 words

The silodosin study reports total prostate symptom-score reductions of 4.59 with added pelvic-floor training and 2.30 with silodosin alone over 12 weeks. The abstract favors combination treatment. These composite changes concern urinary symptoms, not direct measurements of muscle contractility.

### W0058

Question: [IQ0058](question-database.md#iq0058) · Validated FRD: [V0058](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0058) · 37 words

Table 4 of the silodosin trial reports no significant between-group difference in total 24-hour voided volume (p=.851) or mean nighttime voided volume (p=.451). Positive frequency and symptom results therefore do not imply improvement in every diary endpoint.

### W0059

Question: [IQ0059](question-database.md#iq0059) · Validated FRD: [V0059](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0059) · 42 words

The silodosin add-on trial excluded residual urine above 200 mL, neurogenic bladder, diabetes, and several other conditions. It used no invasive urodynamic examination and lasted only 12 weeks. ### W0060

The silodosin study reports daily-void reductions of 1.95 with added pelvic-floor training and 0.90 with silodosin alone. Table 4 gives a between-group p=.003, whereas the abstract’s blanket statement says p<.001. The direction agrees, but the numerical reporting is internally inconsistent.

### W0061

The silodosin trial randomized 158 men to silodosin alone or silodosin plus pelvic-floor training with urgency suppression; 142 were analyzed, 72 versus 70. The paper labels the final set intention-to-treat despite excluding 16 randomized men. This discrepancy limits reliance on that label alone.

## C04 Pelvic floor implementation

### W0062

Question: [IQ0062](question-database.md#iq0062) · Validated FRD: [V0062](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0062) · 37 words

MP75-18 reports referral proportions of 49.5% for pelvic-floor dysfunction, 39.6% for chronic pelvic pain, 28.4% for testicular or groin pain, and 3.6% for lower urinary tract symptoms. These differences describe local referral behavior, not comparative treatment effectiveness.

### W0063

Question: [IQ0063](question-database.md#iq0063) · Validated FRD: [V0063](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0063) · 44 words

The MP75-18 abstract reports 52 men attending at least one physical-therapy visit out of 120 referred. Its text gives 74 scheduled, while the diagram separates 73 scheduled and one outside the window. Referral therefore substantially exceeded observed attendance, with an unresolved scheduling presentation discrepancy.

### W0064

Question: [IQ0064](question-database.md#iq0064) · Validated FRD: [V0064](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0064) · 41 words

MP75-18 reports a mean referral-to-appointment wait of 54.7 days, with standard deviation 37.3 and range one to 215. Fourteen scheduled patients cancelled and eight did not attend. Access delays and attendance losses are measurable barriers before any treatment response is assessed.

### W0065

Question: [IQ0065](question-database.md#iq0065) · Validated FRD: [V0065](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0065) · 41 words

Anderson reports 15 participants with transient minor adverse events, including soreness and occasional trace bleeding, and no serious adverse events. Thirty reported device or readout difficulties. The authors reported no adverse-event withdrawals, but this selected observational cohort cannot establish universal safety.

### W0066

Question: [IQ0066](question-database.md#iq0066) · Validated FRD: [V0066](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0066) · 41 words

Anderson reports median trigger-point sensitivity of 7.5 before wand use, five at one month, and four at six months (reported p<.001). Six-month paired sensitivity data were available for 109 people. This measures tenderness in an uncontrolled pelvic-pain cohort, not urinary flow.

### W0067

Question: [IQ0067](question-database.md#iq0067) · Validated FRD: [V0067](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0067) · 40 words

Anderson lists discontinuation reasons including nonuse, difficulty using the device, hypersensitivity, ineffectiveness, and symptom resolution. Withdrawal was not a uniform failure category. The listed reason counts should not be assumed mutually exclusive or forced to sum to the prose total.

### W0068

Question: [IQ0068](question-database.md#iq0068) · Validated FRD: [V0068](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0068) · 36 words

Anderson reports that 113 of 157 participants completed six months of home-wand use, including 106 men and seven women. Forty-four withdrew for varied reasons. Reported completer improvements therefore do not describe outcomes in all enrolled participants.

### W0069

Question: [IQ0069](question-database.md#iq0069) · Validated FRD: [V0069](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0069) · 43 words

Hall’s supervised programs ranged from one to 52 weeks, excluding home exercise. Eleven to 20 weeks was the most common reported category, found in 21 studies, while duration was unstated in 36. A shared treatment name therefore did not establish comparable exposure duration.

### W0070

Question: [IQ0070](question-database.md#iq0070) · Validated FRD: [V0070](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0070) · 40 words

Hall found that contractions per session, sessions per day, and daily contractions were unstated in 47, 45, and 43 studies respectively. Missing prescription details prevent full reconstruction of the interventions and should remain unknown rather than receive assumed standard values.

### W0071

Question: [IQ0071](question-database.md#iq0071) · Validated FRD: [V0071](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0071) · 41 words

Hall identified eight programs describing different intensities for strength, endurance, or control. Intensity was unstated in 86 studies; hold and relaxation times were unstated in 60 and 69. A generic exercise label often omitted how the intended physiological target was pursued.

### W0072

Question: [IQ0072](question-database.md#iq0072) · Validated FRD: [V0072](validated-frds-clinical.md#v0072) · 40 words

In Hall’s review of 108 male urinary-dysfunction studies, 44 targeted the anal region, eight urethral control, and 21 both. The remaining programs lacked a clear region classification. The common label pelvic-floor training therefore did not denote a uniform anatomical target.
